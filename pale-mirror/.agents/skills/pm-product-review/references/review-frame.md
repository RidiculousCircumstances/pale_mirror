# Living Frontier review frame

## Product promise

A settlement depends on visible infrastructure. The player discovers the
dependency, understands a crisis, changes the region through meaningfully
different choices, sees the physical outcome, and later encounters a
consequence caused by that choice.

## Ironhill causal path

`Mine17 -> vanilla freight corridor -> Ironhill iron stock -> rationing ->
defence -> intervention or evacuation -> aftermath`

Review these choices as complete player paths, not domain commands:

- clear the mine and restore production plus the baseline minecart corridor;
- connect an alternative source through a validated Create route;
- deliver limited physical supplies to buy time;
- prepare a real shelter and evacuate;
- refuse or ignore the crisis and receive a fair, visible consequence.

## Product gates

- No operator commands, opaque IDs, teleport, or developer briefing.
- Atlas owns actionable explanation; Ledger owns detail; JourneyMap owns
  navigation; world cues own discovery and urgency.
- At least four of five unbriefed players explain cause, choices, decision,
  physical result, and delayed connection.
- At least four of five independently discover two choices; the playtest set
  covers combat, logistics, evacuation, and one conscious refusal.
- One co-op audience completes a shared story without duplicated outcomes.
- Positive aftermath is at least as legible as degradation.

Definitions and automated tests are necessary technical evidence, not proof of
these gates.
